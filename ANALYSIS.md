# Analysis: how epub-tts-narrator reads a book

Analysis produces a validated, multi-voice script bundle whose source text and offsets remain traceable to the EPUB. It is independent of the TTS engine and contains no engine-specific voice names, SSML, audio parameters, or audio files. The consensus pipeline, selected with `--strategy consensus`, is extract, discover candidates, resolve identities, check source coverage, design voices, attribute, review, complete, and auditions. The resulting directory can be rendered directly, or a legacy JSON analysis can be exported separately.

## Consensus analysis stages

1. Extract canonical text from XHTML resources in EPUB spine order, preserving chapter membership and source references.
2. Discover evidence-backed characters and narrator observations in independently analyzed source regions. Start with the whole source and subdivide only when capacity requires it, keeping the original source units and offsets. Repeated names in different regions remain separate candidates, not automatically established identities.
3. Resolve split and incorrectly combined identities, ambiguous names, and continuity with prior books through the independent identity audit. Require three independent unanimous approvals before applying an identity change or confirming continuity.
4. Independently check source regions against the proposed cast for overlooked speakers. Process exact-source-anchored findings and recheck affected regions; repeated findings, cycles, or no progress remain explicit unresolved items instead of causing unlimited global passes. New identity proposals receive the same independent review before voice design.
5. Design the voice profiles as one coordinated ensemble over the final resolved cast, before any attribution. See the ensemble section below.
6. Attribute every exact text span with the resulting cast and nearby context. Compact source anchors identify speaker boundaries without echoing the source text. Sparse spoken-spelling edits and scoped delivery/emotion/vocalization proposals can accompany attribution; only those proposals receive a focused independent review. Dialogue and delivery may change inside paragraphs and sentences.
7. Optionally review the completed script for attribution mistakes. Apply a proposed correction only after three unanimous block-level reviews.
8. Find every remaining unassigned spoken passage and request three independent source-based assignments. Apply only unanimous resolved decisions, and retain genuine uncertainty.
9. Prepare an audition script for each voice from clearly attributed speech, joining statements when useful, or ask the model for character-specific casting text.
10. After automatic completion, present remaining choices in a terminal, then validate and save the analysis, cumulative series cast, and renderer bundle. Notes are advisory. Missing performers are not.

Chapter names come from the EPUB's table of contents: EPUB 3 `nav` TOC labels take precedence, with EPUB 2 NCX `navMap` labels supplying uncovered resources. These labels are resolved to their local spine documents; navigation page numbers and landmarks are not chapter names. Reading the TOC does not add it to the narrated text or split a resource into new chapters. Without a matching TOC label, extraction uses the first structural heading, then the HTML document title, then the filename. This avoids exposing a converter's internal names, such as `c114`, when the EPUB supplies a reader-facing chapter name.

A character, narrative or dialogue role, voice profile, and local delivery are separate records. Distinct characters receive distinct profiles. A narrator role may reference a character and share its profile. Profiles separate source-supported traits from creative casting choices; casting proposals are not claims of source evidence. Fresh profiles describe a few compatible audible traits, while temporary emotion, urgency, whispering, shouting, and changes of pace belong to segment delivery. Returning series characters retain their profiles and stable voice IDs through the continuation chain.

Voice design also requests `speechTraits`: one or two short, mood-independent phrases recording the voice's stable speech behavior (the renderer does not send them with each line; accents come from the reference recording and the voice's own chapter history). State the accent explicitly and specifically, such as Glaswegian Scottish rather than Scottish. Where the source establishes no specific accent, choose one as a labeled production decision, considering the supplied cast; neutral General American is an option, not a universal default or factual inference. Describe audible behavior such as clipped consonants, a persistent lisp, or slow habitual phrasing; omit personality and emotions. This field is distinct from the reference-voice creation description and passage-specific delivery. Existing analyses without it remain compatible; they are not silently assigned a new accent. A continuation attempting to add the field only to an inherited profile fails with the defining book and voice ID until that original definition is updated. Existing traits may improve in later books and are carried into subsequent series context without changing earlier snapshots or recreating reference recordings.

Fresh analysis prepares auditions of 25 to 60 words, normally 35 to 50, with a 650-character maximum. Clearly attributed ordinary speech is preferred. Separate statements may be joined with exact source ranges retained. Resolved simultaneous dialogue can serve as a solo audition for each participant. Ambiguous attribution, extreme delivery, repeated cries, and engine control syntax are excluded. If usable speech is insufficient, the model writes original character-specific casting text. That text is marked as a casting choice, never as a quotation or audiobook script, and is saved in `provenance.voice_samples`. Failed auditions remain pending rather than receiving generic text; rerunning analysis or opening casting retries them.

New attribution requests carry a compact cast containing every character, role, and voice ID, identity descriptions, and aliases, but not repeated voice-creation profiles or accumulated cast quotations. The canonical cast and whole-cast ensemble pass remain complete.

Compact responses reference every target unit in order. The first segment begins at the unit start; subsequent boundaries use unique exact source anchors. The application reconstructs exact segment text and offsets locally. Ambiguous anchors, omitted units, reordered boundaries, and unknown IDs fail validation rather than being guessed. Nonempty evidence arrays always contain canonical `block_id`/`quote` objects, including delivery evidence. Compact repairs retain the anchor-only contract and never request copied full segment text. Legacy exact-text responses and validated original compact-prompt caches remain readable through cache-only replay without changing the global analysis prompt version; fresh requests use the clarified prompt. Unknown speakers remain explicitly unassigned, never relabeled as the narrator.

Attribution may attach sparse, source-bound spoken edits for stylized spelling, preserving colloquial grammar, negation, repetitions, names, and intended pauses. It must not copy or paraphrase entire passages as a substitute for source alignment. Bounded repeated-letter edits receive mechanical checks; other edits and new scoped controls receive one independent remove-only review of the proposals and necessary local context, batched rather than replaying whole attribution batches. Rejection or uncertainty leaves the affected wording unchanged. A failed request remains resumable.

Narrator-read assembly additionally proposes deterministic collapses for unmistakable stretched-letter words (`stretch_edits.py`): every attached edit passes the same mechanical check, prefers a spelling attested elsewhere in the book, skips elongated established names, and never overrides a model proposal's span. The consensus path never runs this pass.

Fresh narrator analyses omit publisher apparatus wherever it appears (`front_matter.py`): blocks group into maximal dialogue-free runs, a run qualifies when it carries an ISBN with its number, a copyright mark, an all-rights notice, or two distinct weaker publisher signals (publisher imprint, printing notice, email address, web URL, ZIP-ending address line, trademark notice, all-caps catalog title) in two different blocks, and only the signal-carrying blocks of a qualifying run are dropped. Story prose adjacent to back matter, quoted dialogue, and mid-sentence quoted words never drop. Omitted blocks are recorded as source exclusions, and serial updates never re-run the drop.

Delivery can vary within a single segment. Scoped whispering, emotions, and laughing-while-speaking are ranges; standalone vocalizations and pauses are positioned events. Instructions and short explanations are metadata, never extra spoken words or raw engine tags. A locally supported cue must not become a permanent voice trait or leak into the narrator's text. Source-to-spoken mappings project approved controls through edits and later segmentation changes; unsplittable edits and dependent controls are withheld rather than assigned invented offsets.

When identified characters say the same words together, a segment has a `simultaneous_speakers` list containing each existing role and voice. Its single `role_id` and `voice_id` are null, but the performance is resolved. Multiple candidates do not create a mixed performance. The renderer synthesizes and verifies one recording per performer, then mixes them at the same start time. Current support is unison of the same words, not overlapping speech with different words or staggered entrances.

An empty delivery object such as `{}`, `{"direction":""}`, or `{"evidence":[]}` is completed locally as `{"direction":"","evidence":[]}`. Only omitted empty defaults are supplied. Missing objects, invalid types, unknown partial fields, and nonempty directions without evidence still fail. The same rule applies to proposed semantic corrections before review.

One narrow recovery restores copied repeated-letter counts from the authoritative EPUB. The complete unit must have the same run-character sequence. Runs must be alphabetic, counts must be 2 to 32, at least one count must be 3 or more, the difference must be at most 8, and at most 16 runs may change per unit. Segment boundaries may not split a changed run. Restoration preserves canonical source and speaker assignments, records `source_restoration` metadata, and flags affected segments. It is not fuzzy spelling repair.

Fresh passage tagging uses the shared adaptive runner. Request-budget preflight, explicit provider context-capacity errors, and output truncation can subdivide a batch at whole target-unit boundaries, without a fixed depth or leaf ceiling. Each initial tree is bounded by its original source-unit count. Children retain source context and are revalidated together for exact coverage and order; unrelated failures never trigger subdivision. An indivisible request that cannot fit stops analysis rather than losing source or required cast records. Subdivision provenance records failed parents, validated children, fingerprints, and replayed capacity hints; existing legacy caches remain readable.

## Narrator read (default for new books)

`analyze BOOK.epub -o OUT` analyzes a new book the way a human narrator prepares one. Narrator is the default strategy for new books; select the consensus pipeline explicitly with `--strategy consensus`. A book directory keeps the strategy it was created with, and the two strategies never resume each other's checkpoints. A narrator-read book is also updated from a new EPUB with the narrator read (see [Updating a serially published book](#updating-a-serially-published-book)).

1. **Quotation spans (local, no model).** The book's dialogue style is detected once: double quotation marks (straight or curly), single quotation marks (British style; a quote is told from an apostrophe by position, an opening mark after a space and before a word, a closing mark after the punctuation that ends a phrase), or a leading dash (one paragraph per turn, with the speech split from its tag: `— Hello, she said. — How are you?`). Double quotes win ties, and another style needs at least five paragraphs. Spans in that style are given short IDs, with adjacent explicit speech tags (`"…," Mara said`). Paragraphs without speech are never labeled, only read as context. Unquoted speech such as chat logs is left to the model's explicit-anchor operation.
2. **Prep read (sequential).** The book is read in order in chapter-aligned windows of about 48,000 characters. Each request carries the running ledger (id, name, aliases, sex, short note; narration mode; open questions) and returns only changes: new characters, aliases, exact voice-description quotes, merges with an exact quote, returning series characters (`seen`, with an identifying quote), and identity questions. Invalid optional claims (bad quotes, unknown IDs) are dropped and counted, not repaired. Rows in shapes models copy from the cast lines are still read field by field: a leading proposed ID, pipe-joined fields, aliases after a semicolon in the name, one row flattened into the list, or a paragraph number given as text. An alias must be a short name or title, not a sex code, description or sentence. It also may not be another entry's name: it cannot contain that name, be part of it, or contain its last name word or one of its one-word aliases (so "Lanzecki here" never attaches to someone else). Aliases are checked again against the finished ledger after the prep read. Prior series characters start in the ledger as unseen entries. A prep reply cut off at the output limit (a runaway answer) is read again as the window's two halves, in order. The prep read also records lasting **voice changes** with an exact quote: `age` or `condition` with a short instruction ("about 16 now, deeper") splits the character into states. Voice design writes every state side by side in one request ("Garion (at the start of the book, before any change)", "Garion (later: about 14 now…)"). The state at the end of the book becomes the character's main voice, which later books inherit; each other state becomes a derived voice and role that the renderer clones from the main voice. A character inherited from an earlier book keeps its main voice, and its later states are derived instead, the last carrying into the next book; and an equipment effect (`helmet`, `radio`, `vocoder`, `pa`, `phone`, `underwater`) applies from that paragraph until an `end` entry, or the end of its chapter (an advisory `voice_change` note says where it was closed). Per character at most three identity changes are kept; a moment's emotion, a whisper or a one-scene cold stays a delivery direction. The quote must be about the change: a condition's quote mentions the voice or speech, an age change's the voice or an age/time statement, and an effect's a word for that equipment (helmet, visor, comm, call, loudspeaker…); otherwise the change is dropped and counted (`change_unsupported`).
3. **Identity checks.** A single batched yes/no request checks the merges made during prep. A rejected or uncertain merge is split back into its two entries and recorded as an advisory issue. A second batched check covers suspicious aliases: a separate capitalized name that is not part or a shortening of the main name, and that appears in a paragraph together with the main name. On `n` the alias is split off as its own character, or becomes a provisional sighting of a prior-book character with that name. A new character whose name matches only another entry's alias still gets its own entry. In name lookups a main name outranks other entries' aliases, and a `n` duplicate verdict removes an alias that is the other person's own name. Ledger IDs are plain numbers in one namespace (prior-book characters first), so `12`, `"12"` and `"c12"` name the same entry. A new character whose name exactly matches one prior-book character counts as a provisional sighting only, because a name never establishes identity. So does a prior character labeled during markup. After markup, entries whose names or aliases overlap, where a one-word name equals another entry's first name, or where one name's words are all in the other ("Andurs" and "Captain Andurs"), get one batched same-person check. Only `y` merges them, into the prior-book entry when there is one, and their labels move with them. An unsure duplicate check (`?`) keeps the entries apart, with separate voices, and adds an advisory `identity_duplicate` note. Then one batched check confirms every returning character seen in this book against its earlier description (kept as it was, even when the prep read rewrote the ledger note). It is shown the two paragraphs that name it and the two lines it speaks that share the most words with that description. A character left unsure (`?`) gets one wider look with four of each. When a model's quote is not exact (an elision, reordered text, or its own commentary appended), only its longest verbatim piece of at least three words from a paragraph shown in the item is kept as evidence. The same rule applies to prep-read quotes. Evidence is always exact source text. `y` must come with an exact quote that goes beyond the bare name. It authorizes voice reuse even when the name is shared by several prior characters (`reconcile_series_cast(checked_matches=…)`). `n` makes the entry a new character of this book and adds an advisory `series_identity` note. `?` falls back to the conservative rule: reuse only with an identifying quote and a name shared with exactly that prior character, otherwise review.
4. **Markup (parallel).** Chapter groups of up to about 30,000 characters are labeled with the final ledger, so later evidence informs early chapters. Each request also gets a short read-ahead into the next chapter. The response labels every quotation ID:
   - a cast ID;
   - `-` for non-speech such as titles or signs;
   - `?id,id` when uncertain;
   - `+Name` for a minor speaker missing from the ledger, resolved like a speech tag (exact name, or a first name unique in the cast);
   - `id&id` for the same words spoken together.

   A row may add a thought or letter kind, and a delivery: the direction a director gives the voice actor for the line, in 2–10 words, read from the scene rather than copied from the speech verb. Following Fish Audio's guidance for Drama 3 directions, it combines the feeling and its strength ("slightly amused", "barely holding back tears"), the intent toward the listener ("pleading", "warning him off"), pace and volume ("slow, dangerously quiet"), and a physical state that changes the sound ("out of breath", "through gritted teeth"). Every spoken line gets one; ordinary lines get a light touch ("easy, friendly"). It never describes the speaker's fixed voice (the cast voice has it), soundless movements, or words that do not change the sound ("matter-of-fact", "practical"). A delivery needs no citation when the line and its paragraph make the feeling clear, which is the usual case. The model quotes text only when the feeling comes from an earlier paragraph. That quote is kept as evidence when it is found in the line's paragraph, one of the three before it or the one after it in the same chapter (surrounding quotation marks are ignored), as is an explicit tag containing the delivery word. A quote that is not found is not kept, but the delivery stays. A unison line becomes `simultaneous_speakers` only with source evidence: the model's quote, or narration in the paragraph saying the speakers talk together (in unison, together, both…). Otherwise it stays unresolved for the speaker menu. In-line performance notes are allowed for a quotation or paragraph:
   - a mid-line change, given in the line's own row (`["q12","3","","thrilled","",[["But it means","uncertain, deflating"]]]`, also accepted as one pair or its JSON text) or as a `["q12","then","But it means","uncertain, deflating"]` note: the delivery changes from those words to the end of the quotation or the next change. It becomes a style span that Drama 3 receives as a bracketed direction before those words; chained changes hand over without a reset. A change at the first word becomes the line's delivery;
   - a narration direction, `["7","read","tense, hushed"]`, for a narration paragraph with strong feeling of its own (rare);
   - stylized-spelling edits;
   - whisper, soft, emphasis, stress, forceful, fast or slow spans;
   - pauses;
   - vocalizations. A sound or pause the model anchors on the speech tag that names it ("she laughed") goes on the quotation's first word (before) or last word (after).

   - a per-line voice exception: `["q12","as","normal"]` (a flashback, the helmet is off) or `["q12","as","helmet"]` (an effect for this line only).

   Short italic runs (up to three words, less than half the line) inside a spoken line become emphasis spans from the book's own markup; whole italic lines (thoughts, titles) do not. An emphasis span may sit inside a mid-line change.

   Code also reads how the narration says each line is spoken (`speech_cues.py`), because a model asked to mark this across a long request misses many cases. A clause belongs to a line when it touches the line (the first sentence after it, or the last before it; a single sentence between two lines belongs to the one it is about) and its subject is that line's own speaker: a name or alias, a descriptive alias ("the old man"), or a pronoun that fits the voice's gender ("I" only for a first-person narrator). A clause about someone else, with an unknown subject, or negated ("without laughing") gives nothing. Cues follow Drama 3's documented controls. A sound word (laughed, chuckled, sighed, gasped, sobbed, groaned, panted, yawned, coughed, cleared his throat) becomes a point sound before the line's first word, unless the model already placed one on that line. Only explicit whispering becomes `<whisper>` over the whole line; drawled becomes `<slow>`. Shouted, screamed and hurried cues go into the line's direction as a tone word (`shouting`, `screaming`, `hurried, quick`) unless it already says so. Softly, murmured, breathed speech and speech under the speaker's breath use `soft and quiet`; ordinary breathing actions such as `breathed deeply`, `breathed in` or taking a deep breath give no speech tone. A soft source cue removes a contradictory model-written whisper clause before adding the soft tone. Manner adverbs count only beside a speech verb ("said softly", not "whistled softly"). Thoughts and letters take no sounds or tones. Cues come first in a line's notes, so the book's own words win a conflict, and they skip the review. Counts are in `provenance.narrator_read.segments` (`cue_sounds`, `cue_scoped`, `cue_tones`).

   Each must locate a unique exact span. Code converts the notes to the consensus pipeline's inline-proposal shape and validates them with `attribution_compact.parse_proposals`; invalid notes are dropped and counted, and a note that cannot combine with the row's earlier notes is dropped alone. Mid-line changes and italics have the standing of a line's delivery and skip the review (`narrator_read.prekept_direction`). Every other surviving note goes through the same `review_inline_performance` remove-only review before publication (stretched-letter edits pass its mechanical check). Every target ID must be labeled exactly once, otherwise the usual validation repair runs. Explicit output truncation splits a group in half at a paragraph boundary and retries each half.
5. **Verification (targeted).** One batched verifier gets the paragraph ±2, the options, and their ledger lines for these lines:
   - unresolved labels;
   - labels that disagree with an explicit speech tag;
   - labels that disagree with an adjacent action beat (`"…" Upgrade deadpanned`, or a paragraph opening with a cast name; a beat only nominates, it is never evidence);
   - quotations in a paragraph whose quotations got different speakers.

   - self-check: an **absent speaker**, a character credited with a line although none of their names appears within 30 paragraphs of the same chapter while the text there names other speaking characters (they become the options); a generic speaker such as "spaceport official" counts as named by its own name words;
   - self-check: **self-address**, a line that calls out its own speaker's one-word name ("Killa, wait", "…, Killashandra?"); full-name introductions are not flagged.

   An unsure answer (`?`) leaves an unresolved, tag, beat or mixed-paragraph line for the speaker menu, but keeps the markup label for a self-check line, because a self-check only nominates a doubt.

   When markup and the verifier produce different concrete labels (character versus character, or character versus non-speech), an additional batched pass adjudicates each disagreement. It sees the same source context and cast lines, but only those two labels under neutral option IDs; it must choose one and cannot propose a third. Agreement, an unresolved original, or an unsure or out-of-options verdict does not trigger this pass. The selected label follows the normal validated segment-building path, and `verification.tie_breaks` counts the disputed lines.

   Lines the verifier was unsure about stay eligible for the scene follow-up. Each item also lists the names the text places right beside the line (`named_beside`); an untagged quotation in a paragraph whose other quotations name a single speaker is shown that name, following the one-paragraph-one-speaker convention. Speech tags resolve by exact name, or by a first or last name unique in the cast ("Francu said" for Captain Francu). These hints keep a misleading ledger note from outweighing the text. An answer outside the options leaves the line unresolved. When verification corrects X to Y, the other X lines within 15 paragraphs of the same chapter get one follow-up check with the options X or Y, because one confusion usually affects a whole exchange.
   Before voice design, one request maps the story's origins (nations, regions, peoples, classes) to distinct real-world accents, varied widely so characters of different backgrounds sound different and characters from the same place match across design batches; each voice entry carries its character's accent. Descriptions are natural prose for a designer who has never read the book: real-world terms only, so a description or speech trait naming one of the book's own proper nouns (capitalized mid-sentence more often than not, or a character name) is sent back. Adults get a rough age ("sounds about sixty"). Every voice description begins with its gender, "Male", "Female" or "Androgynous" (only when the text makes the character so); a design without one takes the character's recorded sex ("Male voice; …"), or is sent back to the model when that is unknown too. Voice descriptions say how a voice actor voices the character, by sound: a young character's youth is light, bright, high, quick, never an age or a word for a child, and descriptions never use "adult performer" or sexual wording (hosted voice design refuses them).
6. **Self-check report and cast audit.** A local audit warns (advisory `cast_audit` issues) about a character name that reads like a description or a copied field, and about a name or alias shared by two characters. Each run prints and records in `provenance.narrator_read.self_check` how many lines each check re-examined and changed, the duplicate-check verdicts, the cast warnings, and the lines left for speaker choice. This is how a run reports its own quality without a reference analysis.
7. **Voices.** Batched, terse voice design runs for every non-inherited voice. It uses name, note, exact voice quotes and frequent conversation partners, and returns description, `speechTraits`, and casting-basis traits.

Series continuity uses `reconcile_series_cast` without the consensus audit, with the series check's quote-backed confirmations as `checked_matches`. Segments are built locally: narration outside quotes goes to the narrator, and quotations get their labeled role. `basis` is `explicit` when an adjacent tag names the same character (the tag becomes evidence) and `context` otherwise. Unsettled lines stay unresolved with candidates for the interactive speaker menu; nothing falls back to the narrator. Auditions, speaker choices, and publication then run exactly as in the consensus pipeline.

The configured review notes steer it too, combined as in the consensus pipeline. `cast_review_notes` plus `cast_reconciliation_notes` go to the prep read and the identity checks; `attribution_review_notes` (else `cast_review_notes`) go to markup and verification. They are sent as a `hints` field that the prompt calls fallible leads to check against the text, never instructions or proof. Without notes, requests are byte-identical to a run without this feature, so caches stay valid. The notes used are recorded in `provenance.narrator_read.hints`. The other consensus-only settings (`identity_consensus`, `attribution_review`, `chunk_chars`, `whole_book_discovery`, `cast_reconciliation_rounds`) do not apply.

Every request is cached and validated like other stages, so an interrupted run resumes by rerunning the same command. When the narrator read is used, the provider client requests usage reporting (`stream_options.include_usage`). It records numeric token counts only (prompt, completion, reasoning, cached) per stage, together with wall time, in `provenance.narrator_read.metrics`, alongside the final ledger, merge outcomes and verification counts.

The approach is deliberately cheaper: about one input pass over the book for prep and one for markup, plus small batched checks, with output limited to labels and ledger changes. It drops region-independent discovery, collision waves, identity scans and juries, coverage repair, attribution-review juries, and completion juries. Compare it with the consensus pipeline on the same book before relying on it:

```sh
python3.11 scripts/compare_analyses.py runs/book runs/book-narrator --samples runs/book-narrator/disagreements.md
python3.11 scripts/measure_cached_analysis.py --config epub-tts-narrator.toml --epub BOOK.epub --cache runs/my-book/.analysis-cache -o runs/baseline-measure.json
python3.11 scripts/sweep_narrator_reasoning.py --config epub-tts-narrator.toml --epub BOOK.epub --chapters 3-7 --cell deepseek-v4.1-flash:low --cell glm-5.3-flash:high -o runs/sweep
```

`compare_analyses.py` maps characters one-to-one by co-labeled quotations (one character absorbing two counts as disagreement) and reports per-quotation agreement, unresolved counts, splits and merges. It can write a Markdown sample of disagreements with context for spot-checking. `measure_cached_analysis.py` replays a consensus analysis from its cache with no network to count requests and request/response sizes per stage. `sweep_narrator_reasoning.py` runs model × reasoning-effort cells on a chapter subset, each with its own cache, because cache keys exclude model and effort. The outputs of all three contain book text; keep them in gitignored run directories.

## Character identity consensus

Identity consensus is enabled by default in the consensus pipeline with `analysis.identity_consensus = true`. After discovery and reconciliation, a source scan looks for both directions of error: distinct people combined as one character, and one returning person represented by several characters. Shared or similar names are grounds for investigation, never proof. The audit examines aliases, descriptions, relationships, and source context. A proposed split includes replacement character, role, and voice records before judgment.

Each scan treats supplied cast records and their stored evidence as fallible reference material. Requests use the complete cast when it fits, otherwise an explicitly disclosed scoped view whose included records remain complete. `source_scope` identifies supplied text as a `source_batch` or `complete_source`; a single scan covering the entire book is complete-source regardless of old configuration switches. Every returned evidence quotation must occur verbatim in the matching block's supplied `source_units` text; a genuine quotation stored in the cast is not admissible when that passage is outside this request. One valid local quotation cannot authorize another hidden quotation. A region may reveal a connection to a character introduced elsewhere, and a locally supported concern need not contain all evidence needed for final judgment. An empty scan result means no supported concern in the supplied text, not that the entire cast is certified correct. Missing characters or context alone do not warrant an `uncertain` case.

Batch findings are combined before judgment. Discovery-proposed merges and the independent name-collision screen remain additional candidate sources. Case preparation and all three blind reviewers retain broader source context, including cast-cited blocks and neighboring passages; scan citation scoping does not trim their evidence or weaken exact-quotation validation.

With prior series books, a dedicated continuity scan gives every current character an explicit `new`, `match`, or `uncertain` decision, even when discovery proposed no match. A match requires current-source evidence and the same three-way review. An uncertain decision remains reviewable. An empty current cast needs no request.

Each concrete decision receives three independent blind verdicts. Only three approvals establish consensus. Adaptive name-collision reviews may batch disjoint cases into each reviewer's request, but every case still needs all three reviewers' source-bound approvals. Source-discovered conflicts retain their existing guard; a rejected or uncertain collision cannot be circumvented through a later transitive merge. Matching waves advance approved components and unpaired candidates, retire unapproved pair participants, and never enumerate every similar-name pair. This bounds nomination cases by the number of candidates, without claiming exhaustive identity certification. Accepted changes occur before passage attribution, so workers share one corrected cast. Proposals, evidence, verdicts, constraints, and cast fingerprints remain in `provenance.identity_consensus`.

The casting provenance retains complete discovery and coverage candidate snapshots. After approved identity merges, newly superseded local voice proposals are archived rather than sent for unnecessary voice design or auditions. Their source-supported traits are carried into the canonical voice without rewriting evidence; conflicting trait values remain intact and create a review note. Unrelated unused proposals and established series profiles are not discarded.

Continuity has at most two rounds. A second scan reconsiders every current character when a first-round match lacks three valid approvals, or when an uncertain result has a prior-character candidate. Rejection, uncertainty, error, and unresolved candidates trigger it. A `new` result, or uncertainty without candidates, does not. The second round uses prior feedback only as untrusted leads, performs fresh blind reviews for every proposed match, and enables only final-round accepted matches. The process stops after round two, even if uncertainty remains.

A failed scan or provider request stops analysis without publishing success, while valid cached work remains resumable. Consensus reduces dependence on one answer but does not certify identity accuracy. Outstanding identity notes are advisory; genuinely unassigned speech still requires a performer. Set `identity_consensus = false` to restore discovery and reconciliation behavior without the independent audit, exhaustive continuity scan, or three-way verdicts. No environment variable or separate CLI flag controls it.

Scan prompt and payload changes produce new keys for affected requests without changing the global analysis prompt version or deleting old cache entries. Keep the cache and rerun the same command and configuration to resume an interrupted `analyzed` stage; unchanged requests remain reusable, while affected scans run again. A running process does not hot-reload prompts, and an already-checkpointed `analyzed` stage or published book is not automatically re-audited by this prompt update.

## Whole-cast ensemble design and contrast audit

After identity consensus, source-backed coverage repair, and series reconciliation finalize the cast, and before any attribution, voice profiles are designed as a coordinated ensemble. The whole cast is supplied when it fits; capacity failures split the target voices into automatically sized groups. Each designable voice is targeted exactly once, with character and role context, source evidence, and current `speechTraits`. Identity splits and finalized inherited voice ownership are already applied, so attribution, auditions, and saved fingerprints all see one coherent cast.

Target profiles, traits, and role mappings are never trimmed to fit a group request. Voice-trait evidence is sent in full so an upsert can echo established text-supported traits exactly; character evidence uses the usual bounded exact quotations. Group requests disclose the full cast fingerprint, target IDs, included records and omitted counts; cross-group contrast findings remain read-only advisory context. Upserts cannot edit voices outside the group's targets. If one indivisible target and its mandatory evidence cannot fit, analysis stops clearly rather than dropping that evidence. A cast with fewer than two distinct voices, or with nothing but established series profiles, makes no request; provenance records the skip with unchanged fingerprints, zero request characters, and no updates.

Before subdividing an oversized request, the ensemble pass attempts a lossless encoding of its contrast findings: each voice ID appears in a lookup table, and comparisons with identical kinds and dimension lists share a group of indexed pairs. Every original pair and comparison detail remains present, and all cast records and source evidence are unchanged. The size limit includes decoding instructions. Requests already within budget retain their existing prompt, payload, and cache keys; compact and grouped requests have their own exact prompt/payload keys. Full, uncompressed before/after findings and actual target-group coverage remain in provenance. This does not raise provider limits, output budgets, or weaken acceptance rules.

Only complete upserts of existing local voices are accepted — each carries exactly `description`, `traits`, and `speechTraits` and a brief reason; stored profile metadata beyond those fields keeps its current values. The pass can coordinate casting-basis traits, descriptions, and `speechTraits`; it can never invent, remove, rename, or reorder voices, change character or role ownership, or touch a series-inherited profile: inherited descriptions, traits, and reference identity are reference-only context. Existing text-supported traits must be echoed exactly and may not be changed for contrast, and the pass never claims new text-supported traits — it coordinates casting, it does not discover evidence. The response is fully validated, including a rebuilt-cast check, before any successful cache entry is written, so a cached replay revalidates the complete cast. Changes, reasons, before/after cast fingerprints, and the guidance fingerprint are kept in `provenance.voice_ensemble`. Validated responses are cached and replayed like every other stage.

A deterministic contrast audit always accompanies the pass. It compares recognized audible dimensions declared in each profile's traits — with a few justified spelling and synonym normalizations — between distinct canonical voices only; aliases of one voice never pair. A dimension missing from either profile is unknown, never a difference, and no traits are invented. Pairs with identical known profiles, or fewer than two demonstrably different known dimensions, become advisory potential-confusion findings carrying voice IDs and dimension names. The before-design findings travel with the ensemble request; provenance keeps both the before-design findings and the findings recomputed on the final cast, so a collision the pass already resolved is not reported as unresolved. This is text analysis of profile records: it is not a measurement of synthesized audio, does not prove two voices are confusable, never blocks rendering, and never demands recasting where source evidence genuinely supports similar voices.

Vocal attributes follow a cue precedence shared by analysis and every refresh path: explicit vocal evidence, the user's deliberate choices, and established series profiles come first; specific narrative characterization and story context come second; only when none of those settles an attribute may names, ethnicity, gender, or descriptions inform a tentative creative casting guess. Such a guess is labeled `basis=casting` with empty evidence, its tentative basis is explained in reason/audit metadata rather than in engine-facing prose, and it is never a factual acoustic claim, a universal stereotype, an exaggerated caricature, or a fixed demographic-to-accent mapping. Gender and ethnicity are not acoustic facts.

Accent guidance considers upbringing, languages used, age at migration, and time in the current setting. Fluent English or the absence of an explicit accent label does not default a character to General American. A plausible Japanese or Mexican Spanish influence for a student who moved during adolescence is a creative casting decision; a similarly named person raised in Ohio can instead have a local American accent. Background evidence does not become evidence of an unmentioned audible accent. Local automatic defaults are revisable, while explicit source accents, deliberate user corrections, and inherited voices take precedence. Profile updates keep description, accent traits, and `speechTraits` consistent; a description-only refresh reports any remaining mismatch in its audit reason. The initial narrator seed leaves accent unspecified.

For a small manual model evaluation, `scripts/validate_voice_accents.py --config CONFIG.toml -o /tmp/accent-evaluation.json` exercises original synthetic contexts and records validated proposals plus review criteria. `--stage discovery`, `ensemble`, `profile`, or `prompt` selects a production prompt path; the default is `profile`. `--check-fixtures` is offline. These results test casting choices, not the acoustic quality of synthesized accents, and never modify book bundles.

The casting-description refresh follows the same cue precedence as analysis and carries one consistent `known_cast` snapshot plus that voice's contrast findings.

## Passage attribution consensus

Set `analysis.attribution_review = true` to review the completed script during `analyze`. It is disabled by default because it adds model calls. The review checks narration, speech separated by tags or actions, continuing and nested quotations, direct thoughts, narrator frames, and individual versus collective speech. It uses the existing cast and performance policy. It cannot add identities, change voices, or rewrite source text.

The scanner proposes complete replacements for affected source blocks. Three blind, separately cached reviewers inspect each proposal against source. A block needs three approvals and exact citations. Rejection, uncertainty, or invalid output leaves the original assignment and adds an unresolved item. Confirmed simultaneous assignment can resolve its corresponding unassigned flag. Human corrections cannot be overwritten. Bracketed delivery is reapplied after accepted changes. Source, cast, segments, verdicts, and fingerprints remain in `provenance.attribution_review`.

Every run performs mandatory speaker completion after this optional review, even when attribution review is disabled. It enumerates all unassigned spoken spans, assigns their surrounding complete source blocks through three independent reviewers, and applies only unanimous resolved decisions. Up to three rounds widen context, with the last adding relevant cast evidence from other chapters. Exact coverage, cast references, quotations, and protected human assignments are validated. Completion cannot invent a character or silently use the narrator. Decisions and remaining counts are retained in `provenance.speaker_completion` and resumable cache audits.

Remaining passages are shown separately with wrapped, paragraph-spaced context and assigned voice labels. The interactive menu offers up to three likely speakers, **Enter** accepts the first, a number selects one, **O** opens the full cast, **B** returns, and **Q** or end of input saves and exits with status `2`. Suggestions do not change assignments until selected. Unassigned speech blocks audio generation; advisory notes do not. JEV, when enabled, ranks plausible candidates but does not authorize assignment.

**N** lets you explicitly define a new character and voice, including an identity you know from later books that the current book cannot establish. Enter the name and a self-contained audible voice description. This works when reviewing an already-published book: saving appends only the recorded new character/voice/dialogue-role records, preserves all existing profiles and assets, and updates the book's series snapshot and descendant links atomically with backups. It does not infer identity from the name or automatically merge a separately defined character in another book. Interrupted choices are journaled and can be replayed after a failed save without defining the voice twice. Any missing audition script remains explicit pending preparation.

These are human decisions. An agent running the software must leave the menu and unresolved choices for the user, not enter selections or invent performers to make the book pass readiness.

Rerun `analyze` with the same output directory to resume (an EPUB with new or edited chapters instead updates the book; see [Updating a serially published book](#updating-a-serially-published-book)). Cached validated responses are reused, unresolved blocks receive a fresh bounded review, saved identities and unaffected assignments remain, and unresolved passages are never filled by guessing. Interactive speaker decisions are journaled under the request cache (`speaker-decisions/`) as each is made and replayed on a rerun that reaches the same pre-prompt state, including any voices defined during the prompt; a changed book or cast yields a different journal key and asks fresh. An in-progress analysis keeps a `.analysis-pending/` workspace in the output directory and checkpoints the document after each stage, so a rerun resumes from the latest completed stage without repeating its model calls or local work; the directory never counts as a completed bundle until publish finalizes it. For a pending, not-yet-published analysis, rerunning with a changed EPUB archives the old state and snapshots under `.analysis-pending/archives/` and restarts from `analyzed` with the current source. Compatible cached responses remain available, old snapshots never count as current completion, and an active lease or a strategy/options/prompt identity change still refuses the rerun. A completed book still requires source to match the saved book, apart from new and edited chapters. Conservative narration-boundary repair splits clear tags such as `"Hello," Mara said, "Again."` into character, narrator, character spans while preserving source text. Ambiguous scopes go through completion. Changes are recorded in `provenance.narration_boundaries`.

To resolve existing directories, use `python3.11 -m epub_tts_narrator resolve-speakers ...`. To review an analysis without rediscovery, use `review-attribution INPUT -o OUTPUT`. To re-check a published book's risky speaker assignments, use `verify-attribution BOOK`: it selects the risk set (context-basis dialogue in multi-speaker neighborhoods plus flagged or unresolved lines), freshly re-reads those blocks with the semantic scan and three-lens jury, and prints each suspected misattribution with its evidence. Every ordinary invocation creates a new run-local request cache and never silently reuses a completed earlier audit; its printed run ID supports explicit interruption recovery with `--resume RUN_ID`, which reuses only compatible validated responses from that run. `--chapter N`/`--chapters 3,5` scope the pass, `--sample N` bounds cost, and `--instructions "..."` passes guidance to the reviewers (for example a rule for twins finishing each other's sentences). Operator notes (here, or `attribution_review_notes`/`cast_review_notes` in an `analyze` review) reach the scanner and all three jurors under a notes-specific prompt, together with the current speakers of spoken context lines within four blocks of each writable block (`context_assignments`); they cannot override source tags or context, explicit user choices, the cast, or the output contract, but they decide what the source leaves open among existing roles: a stated turn-taking rule gives alternating half-lines alternating speakers consistent with the neighbors, and a line the source shows as said together goes to all named members as `simultaneous_speakers`. Without notes, scan and jury requests are byte-identical to earlier runs. Any invocation that can publish (an ordinary TTY, `--apply`, or `--interactive`) reserves the whole publication scope — the target book plus its descendants — before the first model request: a marked revision lock whose kernel lease no live process holds is reclaimed automatically, while a live revision lock or an unverifiable/legacy one refuses the run up front with the exact book and lock named. Live renderers never refuse anything: an attribution save is always allowed, the running render finishes the analysis snapshot it loaded, and the next render sees the saved revision. A renderer starting after the reservation refuses itself through the `.analysis-update.lock` handshake, so a long audit or human review can never end in a late lock conflict. Non-TTY report-only invocation takes no ownership and changes nothing. If publication still fails (for example an uncooperative direct file edit), the completed audit stays cached and the error prints the exact `--resume RUN_ID` command that republishes the same findings without new model calls. After printing findings, a TTY offers **Accept all**, **Edit-review** (redisplays each finding and accepts proposals, stages confirmed existing-cast speaker/delivery/review-flag edits, leaves findings unchanged, or finishes; cancel/EOF discards the unconfirmed staged edit), or **Leave unchanged**; EOF and leave are safe no-write defaults. Non-TTY invocation without an explicit mode remains report-only, while `--apply` accepts all findings and `--interactive` explicitly selects findings. Accepted corrections publish through the receipt-bound `attribution` revision scope with full backups and descendant rebinding; the final summary reports applied/unchanged counts, separates accepted proposals from manual edits, and identifies the revision journal after publication. Zero findings require no decision and produce no revision. Review batches retain complete source blocks and chapter boundaries. Truncated semantic scans subdivide up to two levels; jury requests are not subdivided. Invalid scans or provider failures publish no output, while compatible calls remain resumable only through the explicitly named verification run.

`refresh-directions BOOK` rewrites a published book's performance directions and keeps everything else: source, speakers (including unresolved lines, shown as `?`), cast, voices and spelling edits are fixed input. Each spoken line is marked in the text with its ID and speaker (`‹q17 Mara›`, `‹q18 Mara, thought›`); IDs number the whole book's spoken lines, so `--chapter N`/`--chapters 3,5` ask with the same IDs as a whole-book run. The model gets the narrator read's direction guide and writes one delivery per line, mid-line changes, rare narration directions, paired tags, pauses and sounds — no speaker labels, no spelling edits, no voice changes. It is a single pass without a jury: directions are a matter of performance, not a fact three jurors can confirm. The narration's cues for each line's own speaker (see the narrator read's markup above) are applied the same way, and each cued line's clause is sent with its request as `cues`, so the model's direction agrees with what the book says; the prompt tells the model those sounds and tones are already added. In the selected chapters it replaces every spoken line's and narration row's `delivery` and the delivery, pause and sound controls in `performance_controls`. Mid-line changes become style spans and short italics in dialogue become emphasis without review; other notes go through the shared remove-only inline review. Pronunciation controls stay, and a line that has them takes no new in-line notes. The analysis's own `provenance.inline_performance` audit is kept, and each publication appends a counts-only record to `provenance.direction_refreshes`. `--instructions "..."` adds fallible performance hints; without them requests are byte-identical. Every run asks the model afresh, so a run can be repeated for different directions. Its responses are kept only under its own run ID (`direction-refresh/RUN_ID/` in the book's analysis cache), and `--resume RUN_ID` reuses them only to finish an interrupted run. The run prints counts and a few before/after directions, then a TTY asks before saving; `--apply` saves without asking, and a non-TTY run without `--apply` changes nothing. Publication reserves the update chain before the first request and goes through the `attribution` revision scope, with backups and descendant rebinding. It also replaces directions typed in `review-ui`; restore them from the revision backup if needed. Changed lines get new render identities and are rendered again.

`review-ui BOOK` is the interactive counterpart for hands-on corrections: the rendered chapter list opens first — the same chapters the exported scripts use, splitting an EPUB resource at its chapter headings — then passages with per-reader colors and review/unassigned markers, and Enter shows one passage's full metadata. Speaker reassignment ranks existing cast members and offers "Unresolved"; delivery directions are editable within the 240-character bound; performance controls are read-only. Saves go through the same receipt-bound `attribution` revision scope with backups and descendant rebinding, so the cast and every unchanged segment stay byte-identical, and no model calls are made. For rendered passages the dialog also plays the passage's audio and lists its takes: any eligible take can be accepted, including a failed one under the audited `manual_take_selection` basis (original failure report retained, previous acceptance archived, chapter checkpoint superseded — rerun `render --chapters-only` and `assemble` to rebuild).

## Optional corrections

A voice-profile refresh marks every selected profile invalid in `.voice-profile-refresh.json` before the first model request. Each validated proposal is durably checkpointed as prepared, not yet valid for generation. If a model call or publication fails, rerun the same refresh command: matching proposals are revalidated and reused without another model call, independently of the request cache. Changed inputs, guidance, or provider settings invalidate stale proposals. Rendering and casting are blocked while this book or an ancestor has a pending refresh. Successful publication clears the pending state; if clearing fails after publication, rerun reconciles the published fingerprints instead of repeating model work. Reference recordings are not invalidated or deleted. Stable `.lease` ownership files must not be deleted.

To update only voice profiles in an existing book, run `python3.11 -m epub_tts_narrator refresh-voice-profiles BOOK_DIRECTORY --workers 1`. This refreshes voice descriptions, casting traits, and `speechTraits` without reanalyzing source, identities, assignments, or spelling edits. Existing evidence-backed traits, audition text, reference recordings, and accepted audio files are retained. Changed performance traits require new affected speech during the next render, not new reference voices. Original analysis files and audition bindings are backed up in `.analysis-revisions/`; profile publication validates and refreshes descendant continuation links atomically. Process a series one book at a time, starting with the defining book. Parent profiles are supplied to later refreshes so their established accents are not independently reinvented.

When a parent profile declares an `accent` or `dialect` trait, automatic refresh must preserve that complete trait value and include its wording in both the description and `speechTraits`. Validation applies to fresh responses, cached responses, and resumed proposals; compatible pace, rhythm, or other delivery refinements remain possible. This checks declared continuity, not the acoustics or semantic consistency of every sentence. Background alone cannot authorize changing an inherited accent. Use an explicit casting/performance edit or a source-supported analysis correction for an intentional change.

`review` prints source and cast fingerprints, unresolved issues, and flagged segments. Copy both fingerprints into a corrections file:

```json
{
  "source_fingerprint": "COPY_FROM_REVIEW",
  "cast_fingerprint": "COPY_FROM_REVIEW",
  "assignments": [
    {"segment_id": "b000006:0:19", "role_id": "mara_dialogue"}
  ],
  "resolved_issues": []
}
```

Use IDs from the analysis, then apply and validate:

```sh
python3.11 -m epub_tts_narrator apply runs/book/analysis.json \
  --edits corrections.json -o book.reviewed.json
python3.11 -m epub_tts_narrator validate book.reviewed.json --ready
python3.11 -m epub_tts_narrator export book.reviewed.json -o book-bundle
```

Corrections are saved in provenance and leave the original intact unless `--force` is used. A file may also contain a complete replacement cast and exact per-block segment replacements. `analyze --edits corrections.json` replays them only when both source and cast fingerprints match. Stale corrections are rejected, never silently applied to a different identity. See [FORMAT.md](FORMAT.md) for the complete corrections contract.

## Performance policies

Defaults use a known thinking character for explicit thoughts, a known letter author for letters, the current speaking character for nested quotations, and the relevant narrator for headings. Unclear cases require review. Override selected strings in the configuration file; omitted strings retain defaults:

```toml
[performance_policy]
thoughts = "Use the relevant narrator for internal thoughts."
letters = "Read letters using the author's character voice when identified; otherwise flag for review."
nested_quotations = "Keep the currently speaking character's performed voice."
headings_and_nonstory_text = "Use the applicable narrator."
```

Policies guide annotation. They do not alter source wording or bypass validation.

## Long books, resuming, and cost controls

Fresh analysis uses one automatic casting strategy, not a whole-book versus tiny-discovery-batch switch. Discovery groups source units into bounded 60,000-character regions and processes independent regions concurrently up to `analysis.attribution_workers`. A region is bisected over existing source units only when the serialized request budget is exceeded or the provider reports an explicit context-limit or output-truncation failure. Generic timeouts, authentication, quota, transport, malformed output, and schema failures do not cause recursive fan-out. A single indivisible request that cannot fit stops clearly instead of deleting source or evidence.

For `N` original source units, a binary source partition tree contains at most `2N-1` nodes. Preflight-only split nodes make no provider call. There is no fixed depth, partition count, or book-level request quota: larger books receive proportionally more work without operator tuning. Output capacity is observed separately from input character size; character counts are not token estimates. Successful response caches and exact-request split hints let an interrupted run reuse validated work.

Initial discovery does not carry an ever-growing cast from one source region into the next. Local candidate IDs are namespaced by source region, and exact visible source quotations are checked before acceptance. Identity scans and later source checks may use explicit scoped cast views: included records remain complete, omitted membership is disclosed, and the full canonical cast remains intact locally. Scoped views are not permission to merge by name or invent an omitted identity.

Coverage repair follows distinct exact source anchors. A finding is processed once, and only affected source regions are rechecked after a repair; repeated findings or unchanged state terminate as unresolved review items. This proves scheduled source coverage, not that a model noticed every speaker or achieved perfect semantic understanding. The old `whole_book_discovery` and `cast_reconciliation_rounds` settings remain parseable for older configuration files but do not select a separate fresh-analysis algorithm or force repeated global passes.

`plan` reports a baseline estimate, source-derived partition bounds, and conditional work. Identity cases add three verdict requests per case and preparation requests for proposed splits. Prior series books add continuity checks; failed or uncertain matches can trigger the existing bounded second round. Voice-target grouping, coverage findings, speaker completion, repairs, retries, and auditions add data-dependent work. The ensemble request can be skipped when nothing needs design. Pronunciation preparation adds none.

Attribution review adds one scan per review batch and three jury requests for each batch with proposals. Semantic truncation can add bounded subdivision attempts. Initial passage tagging can likewise subdivide a truncated batch. Mandatory speaker completion adds three requests per unassigned source block in each required round, with at most `min(5, attribution_workers)` concurrent requests. Audition preparation adds one request per voice lacking usable source speech and a saved creative script.

`analysis.attribution_workers` accepts 1 to 8 and is the shared concurrency ceiling. Cast discovery uses it for independent source regions, identity review uses at most `min(3, attribution_workers)` concurrent calls, passage review at most `min(5, attribution_workers)`, and these phases do not overlap. Defaults are 12,000 source characters per attribution batch, 200,000 serialized request characters, and one validation repair. Characters are not tokens, and cast metadata consumes context. `analysis.chunk_chars`, `analysis.max_request_chars`, `analysis.repair_attempts`, and `provider.max_output_tokens` bound request size and recovery.

Request retries are disabled by default. `provider.max_retries` enables bounded retries for transient HTTP and typed transport failures. Known quota, billing, authentication, TLS, invalid URL, permanent DNS, unrecognized transport, and invalid-output failures are not retried. Logs omit raw errors, URLs, response bodies, keys, and TLS diagnostics.

On an interactive terminal, model streaming reserves two transient lines: the latest received reasoning excerpt and aggregate progress across active requests (overall step number and step-local percentage, current milestone/count, elapsed time, stream events, reasoning and answer character counts, bytes received, and quiet time). This is presentation only. Reasoning text is never written to logs, reports, request caches, or analysis files, and redirected output keeps the existing plain status lines.

Validated responses are cached inside the output directory at `.analysis-cache/` (or at `analysis.cache_dir`, which must also be inside that output). Cache keys include the exact prompt version, system prompt, and request payload; model, endpoint, and reasoning effort are execution provenance, not key material. Validated responses can therefore be reused when switching models or falling back. Changing request inputs invalidates affected work. Cache entries contain book text and derived annotations, never API keys. Keep the output directory and rerun the same command after interruption. Deleting the output directory deletes its cache and forces a fresh run; a failed analysis does not publish a completed directory. Failed responses remain in `failed/` for bounded repair and are never accepted as successful results.

Resuming the same book needs no flag. `--continue-from` selects prior series context and must remain the same when resuming. Changing timeout, key, retry, model, reasoning, or fallback provider settings does not invalidate completed stage snapshots or validated cached responses; switching models can deliberately mix completed and new work. Changing prompts, cast, policy, or other request inputs can invalidate affected work. Advisory notes do not make a bundle a draft, but missing speakers prevent rendering.

## Optional pronunciation work and targeted attribution edits

There is no independent spoken-spelling scan, pronunciation candidate discovery, CMU suggestion, or name-occurrence classification during analysis. `discover-pronunciations` and `prepare-name-script` remain retired. Targeted, reviewed spoken edits can instead accompany the attribution call that already reads a passage; this does not create a pronunciation registry or a name script.

General pronunciation changes remain optional explicit edits after analysis. A later per-word name/JEV workflow is not implemented. Published books are not automatically reanalyzed or re-prepared on resume. Existing choices, name sidecars, and saved spelling edits remain readable; formats are documented in [FORMAT.md](FORMAT.md#spoken-spelling-normalization). Historical spelling checkpoint labels remain inert so interrupted analyses can resume without restoring the retired scan.

## Bracketed delivery

Square-bracket text such as `[Fireball]` receives a separate delivery cue, including inside dialogue. Speaker and voice stay unchanged. The script retains exact source text and supplies `spoken_text` without delimiters for synthesis. Configure `performance_policy.bracketed_text`; balanced nested and multi-paragraph groups are supported, while unmatched delimiters are flagged. See [the format contract](FORMAT.md#bracketed-delivery). Angle brackets (`<Quest Complete>`) are delimiters too, and an unmatched bracket is never spoken: it becomes a one-character silent group with an advisory review issue, because the TTS engine treats brackets as control syntax. (Bracket delivery version 2; books analyzed before keep version 1 when their cues are reapplied.)

## Updating a serially published book

Books published a chapter at a time (Patreon, Royal Road and similar) can be analyzed as they grow. Download the new EPUB and rerun the same command on the existing book directory:

```sh
python3.11 -m epub_tts_narrator analyze serial-update.epub -o runs/serial
python3.11 -m epub_tts_narrator plan serial-update.epub -o runs/serial   # estimate only the new and edited text
```

The new EPUB is lined up with the saved book paragraph by paragraph, by content rather than position, so authors' revisions are handled too: a typo fix, a rewritten paragraph, an added author's note or a removed paragraph in an earlier chapter. Only appended chapters and changed paragraphs are sent to the model; each edit is read with up to two unchanged paragraphs on each side so speaker attribution sees the surrounding dialogue. Removing paragraphs needs no model at all. The analysis uses the book's own cast and series history as continuity context, so characters who return keep their identity and voice (through the same three-reviewer identity consensus as series continuation), characters returning from an earlier series book reuse that book's voice, and genuinely new characters get new voice profiles designed against the existing, read-only cast. Unassigned speech in new or edited text goes through the usual completion pass and the interactive speaker prompt. The update uses the strategy the book was analyzed with: a narrator-read book updates with the narrator read, whose series check confirms returning characters against the book's own cast instead of the consensus jury, and which leaves unsettled lines to the speaker prompt. `--strategy consensus` or `--strategy narrator` overrides it for one update; the update record's `strategy` says which ran. The output reports the added chapters, each edited chapter's edited/inserted/removed paragraph counts, new characters, returning characters, and how many new voices need casting. The same command with an EPUB that has no new or edited chapters changes nothing.

Every unchanged paragraph keeps its ID and its saved segments, including your speaker choices; the book identity and existing cast records and voices are kept; the update is recorded in `provenance.book_updates`. If the model now reads an unchanged paragraph next to an edit differently, its saved speakers are kept and it is added to the review list (`update_context_attribution`). Pronunciation choices follow their words to the edited text, and a choice for a word no longer in the book is retired. Rendering reuses every unchanged line's verified audio: `audiobook-render render BOOK` casts or designs the new voices, renders the new chapters and the edited lines, and re-encodes untouched chapters' saved PCM only when their chapter-count track tag changes. See [FORMAT.md](FORMAT.md#serial-updates-new-and-edited-chapters) for the contract.

An update is refused, with the book unchanged, when a saved chapter was removed, a chapter was inserted before saved ones, or chapters were reordered (a saved chapter that shares no paragraph with its position), when an edit would divide the book into different chapter files, when extraction settings changed, or while the book is rendering. If a later book continues from this one, an edit-only update that preserves cast and chapter-file layout is allowed: the parent and every descendant continuation link are rebased atomically. Appending chapters to a book with descendants still requires a separate output directory. The update is resumable: rerun the same command after an interruption; checkpoints live in `BOOK/.analysis-update/` and originals in `.analysis-revisions/`. In `expressive` performance mode, prepare the performance plan again for the new and edited text.

## Repairing saved chapter names

After an extractor update, refresh an existing book from its authoritative EPUB:

```sh
just analyze "path/to/original.epub" -o runs/book-01
```

When only chapter titles changed, this takes a metadata-only path: it preserves the text, source blocks, segments, cast, auditions, pronunciation decisions, and speech-cache identities, with no analysis-model requests or speaker prompts. Original analysis files are backed up under `.analysis-revisions/`, and descendant series links are rebound in the same transaction. A book with actual text changes follows the ordinary update workflow instead. Stop rendering before updating a book or its descendants.

For an already completed local render, update the chapter tags and final audiobook from its saved lossless chapter PCM:

```sh
just assemble runs/book-01 --epub "path/to/original.epub"
```

Assembly retags and re-encodes the saved chapter audio and republishes the M4B; it makes no speech-generation or recognition calls for a title-only change. Keep the same render work directory and audio settings. Missing, corrupted, or incompatible checkpoints stop assembly rather than generate speech. For an incomplete book, resume its ordinary render after updating the analysis; its verified matching takes remain reusable. This repair applies to local chapter PCM, not a client holding only downloaded remote chapters. A book-local `performance-plan.json` is rebound with the title revision; an external performance plan remains bound to its original inputs and must be explicitly updated before use.

For a series, refresh each affected book's own analysis in series order, then assemble the completed books. Updating a parent's continuation links does not refresh its descendants' own chapter names.

## Reusing a series cast

Directory analysis saves a cumulative series cast. Continue a series with `--continue-from`; the prior directory stays unchanged and the new output receives its history and a relative continuation link.

```sh
python3.11 -m epub_tts_narrator analyze book-one.epub -o runs/book-01
python3.11 -m epub_tts_narrator analyze book-two.epub \
  -o runs/book-02 --continue-from runs/book-01
python3.11 -m epub_tts_narrator analyze book-three.epub \
  -o runs/book-03 --continue-from runs/book-02
python3.11 -m epub_tts_narrator plan book-four.epub \
  -o runs/book-04 --continue-from runs/book-03
```

Voice lookup searches the current book, then continuation parents. A regenerated voice belongs to the book where it was generated and is inherited by descendants. Missing voices, casting edits, and replacements are written only to the current book. Keep linked directories together.

Author afterwords and explicit “From the Author” sections have a separate author narration identity. An unambiguous matching author can inherit the nearest prior author voice, but this never merges the author with a fictional narrator, guest, or editor. Explicit aliases and existing local recordings take precedence. Tentative gender presentation for a new author voice is casting guidance, not a claim about the author.

Manual sharing is book-specific: `audiobook-render voice-aliases BOOK --set SOURCE_VOICE_ID TARGET_VOICE_ID` writes `voice-aliases.json`, and later books inherit the nearest mapping. `--remove SOURCE_VOICE_ID` removes a local override. Global configuration must not contain `voices.aliases`.

The input directory must contain the `series-cast.json` produced by directory analysis. Directory mode rejects `--series` and `analysis.series_registry`; prior context is selected explicitly with `--continue-from`. Identity consensus requires current-source evidence and three approvals for continuity. Names and aliases find candidates but do not establish identity. Without consensus, matching remains restricted to the sole prior character sharing an exact normalized name or alias, with ambiguous matches reviewable.

### Legacy JSON workflow

For compatibility, `analyze -o book.analysis.json` writes one analysis file. It accepts `--series` or deprecated `analysis.series_registry`, then requires `export` for renderer input. The old registry workflow is:

```sh
python3.11 -m epub_tts_narrator series-import book-one-analysis.json \
  --series casts/my-series.json -o book-one-linked.json
python3.11 -m epub_tts_narrator analyze book-two.epub \
  --series casts/my-series.json -o book-two-analysis.json
```

`series-import` preserves the original analysis and does no fresh identity scan or three-way review. Legacy analysis can update the selected registry atomically after successful analysis. New directory runs do not use `series-import`.

## Extraction scope

EPUB reading order follows the package spine. XHTML is parsed without scripts or network fetches. EPUB 2 and EPUB 3 package and spine structures are supported when their content is parseable XHTML.

- Canonical text collapses display whitespace while retaining inline text and punctuation. Offsets refer to canonical text, not XML bytes.
- Some conversions drop the space on one side of every italic or bold run ("They<i>do</i> need"). When a book glues at least 20 runs to a neighbouring word on one side and keeps a space at no more than 1 in 20 of them, extraction restores a space at every such glued edge on that side and warns with the count. Books that keep their spaces extract unchanged. In a repaired book, genuine mid-word emphasis is split too. An already analyzed book picks up the repair with `analyze EPUB -o BOOK`, which updates only the affected paragraphs.
- Optional `inline_spans` preserve emphasis cues from semantic tags, explicit inline styles, and simple local stylesheet classes. Complex CSS is not inferred. Empty paragraphs mark the next block with `break_before: "blank_paragraph"`.
- Navigation and non-linear spine items are excluded by default and recorded. `extraction.include_auxiliary = true` includes them when present in the spine. Other non-spine content is audited, not appended automatically.
- Footnotes inside selected documents remain in DOM order. Separate footnote documents require deliberate inclusion. Notes are not relocated to references.
- Fixed-layout order, CSS-hidden text, visual poetry, images, SVG, MathML, OCR, and renderer-level pronunciation normalization are not handled as a renderer would. Inspect warnings and exclusions.
- Primary resources without usable XHTML fallback, encrypted content, malformed XML, unsafe archives, and empty extraction fail explicitly. No DRM removal is attempted.
- Chapter entries correspond to selected spine resources. A chapter spanning files or several chapters in one file needs later structural refinement.

These extraction limits are separate from speaker-attribution uncertainty. Review extraction before spending model calls on unusual publications.
